A **text replacement** turns a short spoken phrase into the full text you use often — a signature, an address, a meeting link. Say the shortcut while dictating, and Letterly inserts the full text instead.

Say *“my signature”* → get *“Best regards, Adam”*.

<Callout type="tip" title="Text replacements insert text. They do not fix spelling.">

If Letterly keeps misspelling one word — a name, a brand, a term — add it to your [Dictionary](/dictionary/) instead.

</Callout>

## Open Text replacements

Open **Settings → Text replacements**. You can also reach them from a note through the **⋯** menu.

## Use a ready-made replacement

Letterly comes with a few templates: **email address**, **signature**, and **Calendly link**.

<Figure src="/images/help-center/text-replacements.png" alt="Ready-made text replacements" caption="Ready-made text replacements" />

<Steps>

1. Tap or click **+** next to the one you want to add.
2. Edit the details — for example, replace *Your Name* in the signature with your own.

</Steps>

<Callout type="tip">

The email address template fills in the email connected to your Letterly account automatically.

</Callout>

## Create your own

<Steps>

1. Open **Text replacements** and tap or click **Add new**.
2. Enter the **shortcut** — the phrase you will say out loud.
3. Enter the **full text** Letterly should insert.
4. Save.

</Steps>

Common ones people set up:

| Shortcut | Full text |
| --- | --- |
| *my phone number* | Your full phone number |
| *my address* | Your home or office address |
| *meeting link* | Your Zoom, Google Meet, or Calendly link |
| *quick reply* | A message you send often |

## Choose a good shortcut

- Keep it short and natural to say — two or three words work best.
- Use a phrase you would not say by accident in an ordinary sentence.
- Say it exactly as you saved it. Letterly matches what it hears.

## Where replacements apply

Replacements work on what you dictate — both in your notes and in [Dictation](/what-is-the-dictation-mode/), so the full text appears right in the app you are writing in.

<Callout type="warn">

Text replacements work on what you say, not on what you type. If you type a shortcut by hand in a note, Letterly leaves it as it is.

</Callout>
